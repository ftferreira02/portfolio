## The problem

NEI, the informatics students' association at the University of Aveiro, runs workshops, talks, game nights
and hackathons all year. Quests@NEI turns taking part into a game: students earn points for the
activities they attend, climb a leaderboard and spend points in the association's shop.

For that to work, three things have to be easy. A student has to find what's on and when. An organiser
has to record who turned up while a queue forms at the door. And a student has to see where their
points came from, or the points mean nothing.

It's a team project with several students. I worked on it from February to July 2026, mostly on the web app
(React, TypeScript, TanStack Router and Query, Tailwind CSS, Radix UI).

## What I built

### Finding an activity

I built the activities page: a monthly calendar where you pick a day and see what's on, and a card view
you can search. I later split the large route files into smaller components, which made the calendar and
cards reusable.

### Checking people in at the door

Every student has a personal QR code on their profile. Organisers open a scanner on the activity page and
scan it. I built both, then reworked the scanner for a queue of people: the organiser picks the mode once
(check in on arrival, or mark attendance at the end), and after each scan the confirmation shows the
person's name and points for a moment before the camera is ready for the next one.

### Seeing where the points came from

I built the points history on the profile, which puts points earned at activities and points spent in the
shop in one timeline. The attendance API only returned activity ids, so I extended it to join the
activity names in the database query rather than making the browser look each one up.

### Sessions that expire quietly

Logins expire, and a student with the app open would hit a wall of failed requests. I made the client
renew the session in the background when a request comes back unauthorised, retry that request once,
and send the student to log in again only if renewal fails. When several requests fail at the same time,
they all wait on the same renewal instead of starting one each.

## Where it stands

The platform hasn't been used at an event yet. The association plans to use it, and the team has kept
building it since I stopped working on it in July.
